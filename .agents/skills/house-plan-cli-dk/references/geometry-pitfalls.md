<overview>
Read when repartitioning, reflecting, or diagnosing topology/openings. These are tool-specific geometry lessons, not a mandatory whole-house research loop.
</overview>

<checks>
- Faces are closed orthogonal polygons (rectangles, L and T shapes) derived from wall segments: every room zone must be fully enclosed by wall centerlines (a free interior wall endpoint only virtually extends to the nearest perpendicular wall for zoning, **perpendicular wall by perpendicular wall** — keep the wall short of any face you do not want to split). A corridor whose boundary wall stops short leaves ROOM_FACE_NOT_FOUND; extend the wall or add a perpendicular closing wall.
- An upper-floor room needs no exterior door when a connecting stair run lands in its face: the run must occur on exactly two levels (lower `up`, upper `down`, identical bounds) and the upper occurrence must sit inside a void.
- Circulation connects faces ONLY through doors and physical gaps between walls on a shared edge — windows never provide access, and virtual zoning extensions are not barriers. If all rooms report ROOM_NOT_REACHABLE/ROOM_WITHOUT_DOOR at once, the daily zone was sealed: add a door (even wide, 120–140 cm) on the separating wall.
- Verify `survey.connections[].sides` for EVERY door against its intended rooms; a door's name is not evidence of its destination. Reachability can pass through an unusable 20 cm gap left by a shortened partition. Do not shorten private-room partitions merely to make validation green: require a correctly hosted usable door and a complete privacy boundary. For garage separation, check all physical passages, not just the named separating door; a wide open gap defeats claims of a clean, sealed hall.
- Door swings that collide with furniture flip by authored direction: on a wall authored left→right (+x), `in` opens north, `out` south; on a wall authored bottom→top (+y), `in` opens west (left), `out` east (right).
- Garage doors as variant "single" with swing collide with parked cars; use variant "sliding" with no hinge/swing.
- Open-plan living/kitchen/dining/hall/corridor: use `wall.upsert` with `kind: "virtual"` (dashed line, `thickness: 0`) to divide an open zone into separate room faces (each with its own room seed and area label) while keeping full walkable `passage` connectivity and 0 cm wall-thickness deduction in `netAreaCm2`. Never invent fake interior partition walls with fake doors just to separate open-plan areas, and never enclose exterior terraces with fake walls — author terraces, porches, driveways, roof eaves, columns and entry arrows via `site.upsert`.
- Rotated furniture (`rotation: 90 | 270`): `PlanObject.bounds` swaps `width` and `depth` around `center`. Always verify `[center.x ± swappedW/2, center.y ± swappedD/2]` against adjacent wall centerlines ± `thickness/2` before committing; authoring a bed with `width: 190, depth: 95, rotation: 90` produces a 95×190 vertical footprint on the plan, whereas `width: 95, depth: 190, rotation: 90` produces a 190×95 horizontal footprint.
- Catalog net areas (`netAreaCm2` vs `areaCm2`): catalog cards state net floor area in the clear of walls (`w świetle ścian`), while wall coordinates are centerlines. Check `survey.levels[].rooms[].netAreaCm2` against the catalog card and render with `--area-mode net` when reproducing or comparing against a catalog sheet.
- Rooms merge into one face when an interior/virtual wall is removed but other room seeds remain: moving a room seed alone does nothing — every former room whose seed now falls in the bigger zone merges its face. If two rooms must stay separate after re-layout, author a real or `virtual` wall on the new boundary in the same batch. Conversely, after removing walls and creating a new face, every room label that should belong to the new face must be re-`room.upsert`ed with a seed inside it; the engine does not reassign labels automatically when seeds already exist elsewhere in the new combined face.
- Rearranging a private wing: compute every opening's position from the host wall's junction list BEFORE apply (query report, collect interior-wall vertex coordinates at the host wall's line, subtract wall `a`, place each door strictly inside one free segment). Junctions at segment ends make OPENING_CROSSES_WALL_JUNCTION the dominant failure; offset+width must stay under wall length (OPENING_OUTSIDE_WALL). Windows on a wall with junctions follow the same rule.
- DOOR_SWING_OBJECT_COLLISION is positional: move the object clear of the leaf quarter-sweep instead of moving the door. In the same batch that moves rooms, also remove or re-place objects stranded in the old room positions — they keep colliding otherwise. Warnings (OBJECT_COLLIDES) roll forward; fix only reported errors each round.
- Multi-step re-layout survival: never heredoc-write into the plan file (it silently truncates it); keep ops as numbered files (ops.json, ops2.json…) in a scratch dir and apply serially with growing `--expected-revision`. If the plan file is lost, re-apply the op files in order from a fresh copy — apply is deterministic and rebuilds the same revision chain.
- Staging for a NEW topology: apply level+wall upserts as their own batch first (they pass validation alone), survey that walls-only copy, then author rooms AND openings as ONE batch — a rooms batch without openings fails globally (ROOM_WITHOUT_DOOR / ROOM_NOT_REACHABLE_FROM_ENTRY for every room) and re-applying rooms alone after that never validates. Before authoring openings, walk every intended room seed against the walls-only survey faces: two seeds inside one face mean the partition between them is missing (add the wall or move a seed) — the cheap pre-check for the merge/cutoff failures below.
- Numbers in operations JSON must be JSON numbers, never strings (e.g. `thickness: "15"`): schema validation rejects the whole batch with an opaque side-refinement failure naming only the field — when a batch dies on a field you believed numeric, scan the ops array for quoted numerals.
- After wall.remove, inspect `data.survey` in the `apply --dry-run` response (`survey --plan FILE` of the current on-disk plan does NOT inspect the proposal): a single removed interior wall can silently MERGE two rooms into one face (rooms appear as `['A', 'B']` on one face), CUT OFF a corridor segment so rooms become ROOM_NOT_REACHABLE in the next apply, or leave an orphan face with NO rooms. Fix in the same batch: add a replacement boundary wall at the program boundary, remove accidental leftover wall stubs, and move room seeds if needed.
- After a large room-program reshuffle (swap, garage conversion, wing rotation), budget for a CLEANUP PASS on the batch before it will go in clean: dedupe walls that land on an existing wall's line (a second wall in the same slot splits faces unpredictably), re-seed every room whose old seed was removed (an unnamed face shows as empty `[]` in survey), remove seeds attached to rooms that no longer exist, and delete openings referencing walls that were removed in the same batch (`OPENING_WALL_NOT_FOUND`). Expect 2–4 dry-run iterations; edit the ops file in place and re-run — do not start a new ops file per fix.
- When duplicating a ground-floor wall list onto an upper storey, mirror EVERY enclosure wall including secondary nog segments (e.g. a garage-bay outline that exists only on the ground floor stays unenclosed above): an upper room seeded in a band whose closing wall was skipped gets ROOM_FACE_NOT_FOUND, and rooms behind the broken junction fail reachable. Dry-run diagnostics name the failing seeds; re-derive the missing wall from the ground-floor wall list, patch the ops file in place, rerun the SAME batch on the same revision.
- When moving a room that has an attached sub-room (en-suite bathroom, walk-in closet), compute the sub-room's placement in the SAME decision pass: its only entry is the wall shared with its host, so the host's new position must keep a wall segment available for the en-suite door — check that shared wall's junctions and free segments before committing to the host position.
- Never author a garage/parking space as two orthogonal boxes eyeballed on the render. Compute first: car length ≥470 cm plus ≥60 cm front clearance in the direction of the garage door swing/slide, width ≥190 cm plus door-opening clearance on the driver side. If the interior band is shallower than car length + clearance, the only workable door is on the SHORT side perpendicular to the car — re-check which exterior wall that is, and remember an interior door placed in a new partition wall creates junctions on that wall (recompute neighboring openings' offsets after any new wall).
- Reconstructing a catalog floor plan from a raster image (`przerysuj 1:1`): vision bbox reads drift ±15% m² between runs — compare per-room vs the catalog sheet, but never trust bbox positions alone. Extract walls deterministically with the agent-workflow skill's `scripts/extract_walls.py` (sips→BMP, luminance threshold, density filter, run-length bands): in the 25px window expect ~88% dark (530/600) for walls — 70–75% still catches terrace hatching and dimension chains as false bands; clip the search region to the MAIN plan outline (`--clip`), because right-side legend/room-table panels and bands touching the image border are false detections. Calibrate cm/px from the catalog outline dimension (e.g. the 18,99/19,05 chain) and validate the skeleton with an SVG overlay ON the original (one vision call: "does red lie on the walls"). A gap intended as an open passage does NOT create reachability — windows never grant entry; a terrace opening must be a sliding DOOR or the room stays ROOM_NOT_REACHABLE. Opening offset must be strictly >0 (schema predicate) and offset+width ≤ wall length; a second wall on the same slot splits faces unpredictably — prefer one wall with an inline junction (shorten+second wall only as last resort). CLI Y-up flips the rendered plan N/S vs the source image — expect it in comparisons.
- After the walls exist, build the room-box grid NORMALIZED: shared edges of adjacent rooms must be EXACTLY the same coordinate (no ≤30 cm tolerance merging when fusing neighbor edges). Sub-30 cm edge offsets create sliver faces, and room claims then ride on shifted faces — the symptom is `valid=true` while per-room `areaCm2` is wildly off the card (totals alone look plausible). Verify per-room `areaCm2` against the catalog card on the FIRST survey after apply; fix by snapping BOTH neighbors to one shared coordinate, never by nudging one edge (the next room inherits the offset). Resolve every opening host against the FINAL post-normalization wall set, never the pre-normalization draft (pre-normalization resolution silently binds to slots that no longer exist at apply time → OPENING_OUTSIDE_WALL on a wall that "is right there"); never host on a segment ending at a junction (OPENING_CROSSES_WALL_JUNCTION); offset is strictly >0 (schema predicate rejects 0) and offset+width ≤ wall length. Dry-run first: it validates schema + geometry without advancing the revision, so iterating a rejected batch is cheap and the accepted one commits unchanged.
- OCR beats vision for label geometry: `tesseract <img> out -l pol+eng --psm 11 tsv` returns every room label + area with a pixel box, deterministic every run. Vision bbox estimates for the same labels drifted ±15% between passes and missed two 11,19 m² rooms hidden under a pasted-in legend that OCR of the clean catalog image found at once. Use vision for exactly one overlay check ("do the red bands lie on walls"), never as the measuring instrument.
- Erosion, not density threshold, separates walls from hatching: a separable 9×9 erosion keeps only runs ≥9 px (walls), dropping terrace hatching, dimension chains and furniture strokes; a 5×5 erosion still keeps furniture. Density-threshold banding has both failure modes at once — phantom hatch bands at 70–75% and lost thin partitions (10–12 px) above 85%.
